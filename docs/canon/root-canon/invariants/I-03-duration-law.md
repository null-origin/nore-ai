# **Invariant I-03 — Duration Law**

### **Status:** Canonical Invariant (Frozen)

### **Applies To:** Root Canon v1 • Cycle Timing • Resistance Mechanics

---

## **Invariant Statement**

The duration of a cycle is determined solely by the resistance present within its radius band. Time does not advance cycles; cycles conclude only when resistance drops below threshold. Apparent temporal length is an effect of resistance density, not of chronology or intention.

Low-radius bands sustain higher resistance and therefore longer cycle durations. High-radius bands sustain less resistance and therefore shorter, more compressed cycles. Identical numbers of cycles may occupy radically different spans of calendar time.

---

## **Formal Definition**

Let **Dₙ** represent the duration of cycle *n*, and **Rₙ** represent the radius band in which cycle *n* operates.

**Dₙ ∝ Resistance(Rₙ)**

As resistance approaches zero, duration collapses toward instantaneous transition. Where resistance is dense, duration extends and time appears viscous.

---

## **Operational Laws**

1. **Non-Temporal Causality** — The passage of time never causes a cycle transition; resistance exhaustion does.
2. **Resistance Density** — Lower radius bands contain denser, more distributed resistance and therefore longer durations.
3. **Compression Effect** — As radius increases, resistance thins and cycles compress.
4. **Instantaneity Threshold** — When resistance collapses completely, transition occurs without measurable duration.
5. **Domain Invariance** — The Duration Law applies equally across psychological, relational, institutional, and macro domains.

---

## **Canonical Notes**

* Duration must never be forecast by calendar time alone.
* Apparent acceleration is always the result of resistance thinning, not increased activity or intent.
* Any interpretation that treats duration as linear, earned, or controllable contradicts this invariant.
