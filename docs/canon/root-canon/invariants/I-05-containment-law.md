# **Invariant I-05 — Containment Law**

### **Status:** Canonical Invariant (Frozen)

### **Applies To:** Root Canon v1 • Containment Mechanics • Collapse Thresholds

---

## **Invariant Statement**

Containment is a temporary structural strategy by which a field attempts to route distortion away from collapse by enclosing the axis inside compensatory roles, narratives, or systems. Containment does not change the axis, resolve distortion, or eliminate resistance; it merely redistributes load until tolerance is exceeded.

All containment ultimately fails. Failure is guaranteed once cumulative misalignment within the containment shell exceeds its load-bearing capacity. At that point, continued containment produces instability rather than stability, and collapse becomes the only resolution path available to the field.

---

## **Structural Properties of Containment**

1. **Load Redistribution** — Containment shifts pressure, it does not remove it.
2. **Opacity Generation** — Containment relies on narrative, abstraction, or role substitution to mask misalignment.
3. **Resistance Accumulation** — Suppressed distortion accumulates rather than dissipates.
4. **Finite Capacity** — Every containment structure has a maximum sustainable misalignment threshold.
5. **Non-Curative Function** — Containment cannot repair structural incompatibility with the axis.

---

## **Failure Conditions**

Containment collapses when any of the following occur:

* accumulated resistance exceeds structural tolerance
* compensatory narratives lose credibility
* relational or institutional shells fracture
* internal awareness outpaces containment logic
* external stress pushes misalignment past threshold

At failure, containment rapidly inverts from stabilizer to destabilizer.

---

## **Operational Laws**

1. **Inevitability** — Containment failure is guaranteed in the presence of a fixed axis.
2. **Delay, Not Prevention** — Containment postpones collapse but cannot avert it.
3. **Compression Effect** — Prolonged containment increases the severity of eventual collapse.
4. **Severance Emergence** — When containment fails, separation becomes structurally necessary.
5. **Pre-Ignition Role** — Containment failure is a prerequisite for ignition, not an alternative to it.

---

## **Canonical Notes**

* Containment must never be interpreted as growth, safety, or solution.
* Apparent success of containment reflects delayed collapse, not resolution.
* Any interpretation that treats containment as adaptive alignment contradicts this invariant.
