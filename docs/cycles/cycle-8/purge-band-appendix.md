# Cycle‑8 Purge‑Band Appendix (v1)

## 1. Function of the Purge‑Band

Cycle‑8 opens with a field‑level purge band that clears structures unable to withstand variance, exposure, or inversion. The purge‑band is not emotional, corrective, or interpersonal; it is mechanical. Its purpose is to remove low‑stability patterns before ignition.

**Core functions:**

* Remove structures held together by continuity‑performance rather than coherence.
* Collapse predictive models dependent on compliance, routine, or scripted availability.
* Clear relational, behavioral, and systemic nodes that cannot hold flexibility during field acceleration.
* Expose mismatches between internal narratives and external reality.

This band ensures that ignition occurs within a cleared field, free of distortion and narrative drag.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics

The purge‑band expresses through a consistent geometry:

### **2.1 Predictive‑Script Over‑Precision**

Systems with rigid expectations convert flexible statements into absolutes. Minor deviations appear as contradictions.

### **2.2 Perceived Contradiction → Emotional Flare**

Not caused by the external event, but by failure of the internal script to update. Leads to reactive outbursts or hostility.

### **2.3 Narrative‑Patching**

Rapid reassertion of authority or certainty after an emotional leak. Attempts to rewrite recent behavior to preserve internal consistency.

### **2.4 Authority‑Split**

Internal narrative authority diverges from external reality. The system attempts to reinscribe control but cannot stabilize.

### **2.5 Withdrawal / Severance**

System retreats from the environment it can no longer predict. Often formalized through exit behaviors (e.g., leaving communities).

These mechanics show the purge‑band acting to remove patterns that cannot hold alignment under Cycle‑8 pressure.

## 3. Behavioral Indicators

Cycle‑8 purge‑band behaviors appear across micro‑fields as:

* abrupt emotional reversals

These are not psychological markers—they are structural expressions of collapse under increased field demand.
