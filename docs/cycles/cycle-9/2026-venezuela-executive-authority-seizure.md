# U.S. Seizes Venezuelan Executive Authority in Narco-Terrorism Operation, Signals Interim Governance and Energy Reordering

The United States has carried out a large-scale military and law-enforcement operation inside Venezuela, resulting in the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, and marking the most direct assertion of U.S. authority over the country in decades.

U.S. officials said the operation was conducted pursuant to a long-sealed federal indictment unsealed in the Southern District of New York, charging Maduro, Flores, close family members, and senior political and military officials with narco-terrorism conspiracy, large-scale cocaine trafficking, and weapons offenses. Prosecutors allege that for more than 25 years, senior Venezuelan officials corrupted state institutions to facilitate the shipment of hundreds of tons of cocaine into the United States, partnering with foreign terrorist organizations and major transnational drug cartels under what investigators describe as the “Cartel de los Soles.”

According to U.S. authorities, Maduro used every major office he held—from legislator to foreign minister to president—to protect and expand the trafficking network, providing diplomatic cover, military protection, and logistical support for cocaine shipments routed through Venezuela. The indictment also details the alleged use of state aircraft, military-controlled airports, maritime routes, and the sale of diplomatic passports to traffickers.

The capture operation, which U.S. officials described as a coordinated military-law-enforcement action, involved elite U.S. forces and resulted in Maduro and Flores being removed from Venezuela and transferred to U.S. custody. U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi said the defendants will face trial in New York, where prosecutors assert jurisdiction based on the international scope of the conspiracy and the presence of at least one defendant in U.S. custody.

President Donald Trump publicly confirmed the operation, calling it a decisive action against what he described as a narco-terror organization that had “taken possession of a country.” Trump said the United States acted to protect its personnel and enforce U.S. law, while senior administration officials reiterated long-standing claims that Maduro’s government lacked democratic legitimacy.

In statements following the operation, Trump went beyond a law-enforcement framing and declared that the United States would temporarily oversee Venezuela’s governance until what he described as a “safe, proper and judicious transition” could be achieved. He said U.S. authorities would work with designated Venezuelan officials and international partners during an interim period, though no detailed governance structure was immediately disclosed. The administration acknowledged that Congress was not briefed in advance, drawing criticism from some lawmakers who questioned the constitutional and international-law basis for the action.

International reaction was mixed. Some U.S. lawmakers defended the operation as falling within the president’s inherent authority to protect U.S. personnel and enforce federal law, while others warned that unilateral military action without congressional authorization risked repeating past U.S. interventions with destabilizing consequences. Regional leaders called for emergency consultations at international organizations, and Venezuelan state media reported explosions and military activity around Caracas during the operation.

Alongside the political and military developments, Trump signaled a rapid economic reorientation for Venezuela, stating that major U.S. oil companies would invest billions of dollars to repair and expand the country’s degraded energy infrastructure. He said the United States would oversee the reopening of Venezuela’s oil sector under existing sanctions, with companies reimbursed for reconstruction costs, and framed the move as a way to restore production from the country with the world’s largest proven oil reserves. U.S. oil majors operating in Venezuela said they were focused on employee safety and compliance with applicable laws, while analysts noted the continued presence of Chinese and Russian interests in the sector.

The operation caps months of escalating U.S. pressure on Maduro, including oil sanctions, maritime interdictions, and public warnings that his removal was imminent. With Venezuela’s head of state now in U.S. custody, the episode represents a decisive shift from sanctions and diplomatic isolation to direct intervention—collapsing the distinction between law enforcement, military action, and interim governance, and opening a new and uncertain phase for Venezuela’s political and economic future.
